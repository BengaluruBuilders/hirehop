# 06 - Distribution, Acquisition, Retention, and Validation

Product: TailorMyResume (native Android AI job application assistant for Indian freshers).
Report date: 2026-09-30.
Author: research agent 6 of 6.

## 0. How to read this report

- Source grades: A = official or primary data. B = reputable press, analyst, or company data about itself. C = forum, anecdote, agency or vendor blog.
- Most cost and channel numbers come from grade C sources. Treat them as ranges to test, not as facts.
- Marked "ESTIMATE" means the number comes from my own arithmetic or assumption. No source states it.
- Marked "STALE" means the data is older than 12 months.
- Research gaps are in section 12. Read them before you trust the tables.

## 1. Summary of findings

1. Paid acquisition does not fit a low-price, episodic product. A Meta or Google install costs about INR 13 to 60 (grade C). A payer costs INR 1,000 to 3,000 at a 1% to 2.6% install-to-paid rate (ESTIMATE). A pack at INR 99 to 199 cannot pay for that.
2. Organic and community channels must carry launch. The best fit is college WhatsApp and Telegram groups, T&P (placement) officers, founder-led LinkedIn, and small student-community pages.
3. The Play Store head terms are taken. "Resume builder" apps hold 5M to 50M+ installs (grade A, observed on Play listings today). TailorMyResume must win long-tail terms: JD match, tailored resume, cover letter for freshers, application tracker.
4. Retention will be episodic. Cross-industry Day 30 retention is 5% to 7% (grade B/C). No source gives a job-app benchmark. Design for repeat packs and referral, not for daily use.
5. Timing matters. Today (30 Sep 2026) is the start of the Sep to Nov off-campus peak. The next peak is Jan to Apr 2027 (mass IT recruiters). A test that runs in October uses real demand.
6. Google Play blocks a fast launch. A new personal account needs 12 testers opted in for 14 days, then a review of up to 7 days. Plan at least 4 to 5 weeks from first testable build to public release.
7. A concierge pre-sale is the cheapest valid test. Budget: INR 10,000 (cap INR 12,000). Duration: 4 weeks. The main signal is real money paid for one tailored application pack.

### 2.1 Channel notes

- WhatsApp groups: Do not spam. Ask a known member to post one message. Offer a free JD gap check. Add a consent notice before you collect a phone number (see section 9).
- T&P sessions: Offer a session on "How to read a job description and fix your resume for it." Give each student one free check. The T&P officer then sees value and repeats the invitation.
- LinkedIn: Post one real teardown each week. Remove all personal data. Ask permission first.
- Creators: Ask for an honest review. Do not offer money to claim "guaranteed ATS pass". TailorMyResume must not make that claim.

### 3.1 Unit economics check (ESTIMATE)

Assumptions:
- Pack price INR 149. Play service fee 15% (first USD 1M per year; from my background knowledge, not re-verified here). Net INR 127. Check GST separately.
- Install cost INR 30 (Meta) or INR 40 (Google).

| Install-to-paid rate | Cost per payer at INR 30 per install | Cost per payer at INR 40 per install |
|---|---|---|
| 2.6% (Business median, global) | INR 1,154 | INR 1,538 |
| 1.0% (India Android guess) | INR 3,000 | INR 4,000 |
| 24% or more (needed to break even) | INR 125 | INR 167 |

Result: Paid installs lose money by a factor of 9 or more on one pack. Break-even needs a 24% install-to-paid rate. No benchmark supports that. Paid acquisition works only if one user buys many packs or a much higher-price plan exists. Test that first with the concierge run.

Reading: Users have an active window of 1 to 6 months. After they get a job, they leave. Do not plan for Day 30 retention above 5% to 8%.

### 5.2 Retention risk and mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Users leave after one pack or after they get a job. | Sell packs (per use), not a subscription. Revenue then matches the use pattern. |
| Google Play subscriptions lose about one third of cancellations to billing failure (RevenueCat). | Avoid subscriptions in MVP. If you add "active-job-search plan", sell it as a fixed 30 or 60 day pass with UPI. |
| Users come back only in placement season. | Send re-engagement in Sep to Nov and Jan to Apr: "New drive: tailor your resume in 2 minutes." Use WhatsApp opt-in, not only push. |
| Placed users have no reason to stay. | Keep the profile as a "career vault". Prompt at natural events: first appraisal (12 months), first switch (18 to 24 months). This is a hypothesis to test, not evidence. |
| Each batch leaves after 1 year. | Ask placed users to pass TailorMyResume to juniors. Next year's batch is the growth source. |
| Weak habit loop. | Track applications per user per month, not sessions. Add the job tracker with status reminders. |

### 5.3 Metrics to track (instead of Day 30)

- Packs per active user in 30 days. Target: 1.5 or more.
- Repeat purchase within 30 days. Target: 30% or more.
- Referral rate: new users per existing user. Target: 0.3 or more.
- Returned users in the next season (Jan to Apr for Sep to Nov users). Target: 15% or more (ESTIMATE, no benchmark).

Campus phases in the 2026 to 2027 cycle:
- Phase 1 (Jul to Sep): top product firms at top colleges (IIT, NIT, IIIT, BITS, tier-1 private).
- Phase 2 (Oct to Dec): mid-tier product firms, GCCs, funded startups.
- Phase 3 (Jan to Apr 2027): mass IT recruiters (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini).

Demand context (grades B/C):
- 73% of employers planned fresher hiring in Jan to Jun 2026 (TeamLease EdTech survey of 1,051 employers, Nov 2025 to Jan 2026).
- About 84% to 85% of undergraduates or engineering students had no offer in Jan to Feb 2026 (Unstop survey of 37,000+ students).
- Naukri JobSpeak: fresher (0 to 3 years) hiring grew about 16% year on year by Mar 2026. Non-IT sectors (BPO, insurance, retail, relationship manager roles) grew fastest.
- Mass IT recruiter absolute hiring is down about 18% year on year (OwnYourCareer, C).
- Off-campus hiring is now the main route for students outside top colleges (C).

Implication: The target user is a fresher from a tier-2 or tier-3 college who applies off-campus. Non-IT roles also grow. Do not limit the product to software roles.

Academic-year note (general knowledge, not sourced): final-year exams run Apr to May, results May to Jul, and pass-out batches start job search in Jun to Jul.

## 7. Referral and virality mechanics

Evidence found is thin. Indian student apps mostly use reward-based referral (extended subscription or discount). Unacademy pays rewards only after a friend buys a subscription of at least INR 5,000 for 6 months or more (help center, B). That model does not fit a low-price pack.

I found no data on "share your resume" loops. The ideas below are hypotheses. Test each one.

| Idea | Mechanic | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pack credit swap | User gets 1 free pack credit when 2 friends finish a first JD analysis. | About INR 5 to 15 of AI cost per free pack (ESTIMATE) | Fake accounts. Cap at 3 credits per user. Require a verified phone. |
| Shareable JD-fit card | Image with a match score and "3 gaps to fix", made for WhatsApp Status. It shows no resume text. | Low | Do not claim ATS results. Say "estimated match". |
| Placement-group code | Each college group gets a code. The group with the most tailored applications gets a public thank-you. | Low | Leaderboards can pressure users. Keep it opt-in. |
| Senior-to-junior pass | Placed users get a link to give juniors a free pack. | Low | Needs a placement trigger. Ask "Got a job?" in the tracker. |
| Ambassador links | 10 to 20 student partners get tracked links and free packs. Pay INR 20 to 30 per paid pack (ESTIMATE). | Medium | Quality control. Internshala says the model needs a dedicated team. |
| Resume footer link | "Made with TailorMyResume" on the exported PDF. | Free | Recruiters may dislike it. Make it optional and off by default. |

Rule: Never reward false claims. Never let a referral reward change resume content.

## 8. Validation test design

### 8.1 Goal

Prove or reject one belief: Indian freshers will pay INR 99 to 199 for a tailored application pack (resume wording, cover letter, gap list, prep questions for one job description), delivered fast, with no invented facts.

### 8.2 Test 1: Concierge pre-sale (main test)

| Item | Design |
|---|---|
| Audience | Final-year students and 0 to 1 year pass-outs in B.Tech, BCA, MCA, BBA, MBA, B.Com. Focus on tier-2 and tier-3 colleges. They apply off-campus now. |
| Channels | 5 to 8 college WhatsApp or Telegram groups through personal contacts. 2 to 3 T&P or faculty sessions. Founder LinkedIn posts (2 per week). 2 to 3 student-community pages (barter or small fee). Meta ad test with a small budget. |
| Offer | Step 1 (free): "JD gap check." The user sends a resume and one job description on a Google Form or WhatsApp. The founder returns a gap summary within 24 hours. Step 2 (paid, INR 149): Application pack. Tailored resume text, cover letter, 10 prep questions, delivered within 24 hours. Payment by UPI link. |
| Production | Founder plus an AI model. Same rules as the product: use only real experience. Ask the user to confirm each change. Never claim ATS acceptance or job outcomes. |
| Duration | 4 weeks of selling (5 Oct to 1 Nov 2026), plus up to 2 weeks to deliver and survey. |
| Consent | Show a short consent notice on the form. State the purpose. Delete data on request. See section 9. |

Budget (INR, ESTIMATE):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Landing page and domain (free builder plus domain) | 1,500 |
| Meta ads to the landing page | 5,000 |
| Student-community page shoutouts (2 to 3, negotiate) | 2,500 |
| AI model and tools | 1,000 |
| Total plan | 10,000 |
| Contingency (cap) | 2,000 |
| Hard cap | 12,000 |

Ad budget note: at INR 20 to 50 per landing-page lead (ESTIMATE, no source), INR 5,000 buys 100 to 250 leads. The test measures the real cost.

Metrics and thresholds:

| Metric | Green (success) | Amber | Red (failure) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing visitors (all sources) | 300 or more | 150 to 299 | Under 150 |
| Free JD gap check requests | 120 or more | 50 to 119 | Under 50 |
| Paid packs at INR 149 | 12 or more, and at least 10% of gap-check recipients | 5 to 11 | Under 5, or under 4% |
| Repeat: second pack or bundle within 14 days | 30% or more of buyers | 10% to 29% | Under 10% |
| Referral: new requests through a code per recipient | 0.2 or more | 0.1 to 0.19 | Under 0.1 |
| Buyer rating (1 to 5, median) | 4.0 or more | 3.5 to 3.9 | Under 3.5 |
| Buyers who use the output in a real application in 7 days | 70% or more | 50% to 69% | Under 50% |
| Fabricated claims found in output | 0 | 1 (fix and disclose) | 2 or more |
| Founder time per pack (after the 20th pack) | 20 minutes or less | 21 to 45 minutes | Over 45 minutes |

Sample size note: 12 to 25 orders is a small sample. It supports a go or stop decision. It does not support a revenue forecast.

Also collect in the post-delivery survey (3 questions): "At what price is this too cheap to trust? At what price is it too expensive? What would you use it for next?"

Decision map:

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Green on requests, paid packs, quality, and time | Build the Android MVP. Price packs at INR 99 to 199. Invite buyers to the closed test. |
| Requests Green, paid packs Amber | Run 2 more weeks. Try INR 99, a bundle of 3 (INR 299 to 349), or a narrower niche (for example IT freshers only). |
| Requests Green, paid packs Red | Users want the help but not the price. Drop the paid-pack hypothesis. Test a free tool with a sponsor or a college (B2B) model, or stop. |
| Requests Red (any channel mix) | The channel or message failed. Change the message and channel once. If it fails again, stop. |
| Paid Green, repeat Red | Packs work as one-time sales. Plan for high acquisition need. Do not build a plan or subscription. |
| Quality or fabrication Red | Fix the generation rules before any build. This is a product risk, not a demand risk. |

### 8.3 Test 2: Waitlist for the Android app (parallel)

- Place a waitlist form on the same landing page: "Get early access on Android. Enter your WhatsApp number."
- Collect consent for WhatsApp messages.
- Success: 150 or more signups in 4 weeks, with at least 40% from non-paid sources.
- Failure: under 60 signups.
- Use: seeds the Play closed test and the pre-registration audience.

### 8.4 Test 3: Closed test (starts when the first build exists)

- Invite 40 from the waitlist. Success: 15 or more opt in (about 35%). Failure: under 10.
- Success for engagement: at least 10 testers open the app on 5 or more of the 14 days.
- This test also meets the Play production access rule (section 10).

### 8.5 Why not a fake-door paid pack on Play

- A Play listing that shows a purchase that does not work may break the Deceptive Behavior policy (A: apps must perform as users expect). Risk of suspension on a new account is high. This is my reading, not a ruling.
- A pre-sale outside the app (UPI link, real delivery) gives a stronger signal and no policy risk.
- If you later sell inside the app, Google Play Billing is required for digital goods. Check this before you add in-app payment.

## 9. Legal note for the test

- India's DPDP Rules were notified on 14 Nov 2025. Consent Manager rules start about Nov 2026. Most other duties start about May 2027 (18-month phase).
- You collect resumes, phone numbers, and job data. Give a clear, standalone consent notice from the first day. This is good practice now and will be required later.
- Do not store resumes longer than needed. Delete on request. Keep a simple log.
- This is not legal advice. Ask a lawyer before launch.

## 11. Implications for the PRD

1. Price and packaging. Use per-use application packs (INR 99 to 199). Do not build a subscription in MVP. Add a fixed-length "active-job-search pass" only after pack repeat data exists.
2. Payments. Use Google Play Billing for in-app packs. Show UPI-friendly prices. Localize prices for India.
3. Acquisition. Build for community distribution. The product needs a shareable output (JD-fit card), a referral credit, and a college or group code.
4. Onboarding. First value must come in under 3 minutes: paste a job description, see the gap list. Do not force account creation before this. This helps closed-test engagement.
5. Free tier. Keep the JD gap analysis free. It is the hook for WhatsApp and LinkedIn content and the top of the funnel.
6. Job tracker. Keep it in MVP scope. It gives a reason to return in the same season and a place for status reminders.
7. Re-engagement. Add opt-in WhatsApp or push reminders that follow the placement calendar.
8. Play readiness. Plan 4 to 5 weeks for closed test and review. Put "Play account, ID, device check" on the critical path now.
9. Trust rules. Keep "no invented qualifications" and "no ATS guarantee" as hard product rules. They also protect the Play listing from Deceptive Behavior claims.
10. Scope of roles. Do not limit templates and prompts to software roles. Non-IT fresher hiring grows fastest.
11. Metrics. Track packs per user, repeat rate, referral rate, and season return. Do not use Day 30 retention as the main goal.
12. Privacy. Add consent notice, data deletion, and short retention from day one (DPDP).
13. Timing. Start the validation test now (Oct 2026). Target public launch between Jan and Feb 2027, inside the mass-recruiter season.
