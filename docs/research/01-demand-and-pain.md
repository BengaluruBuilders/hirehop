# 01 - Demand and User Pain

Date of research: 2026-09-30
Scope: pains of fresh graduates and early-career candidates in job applications. India first, then US.

## How to read the grades

- A = primary data or survey, or first-party platform data.
- B = reputable press or analyst that reports a primary source.
- C = forum post, anecdote, vendor blog, or SEO data with no method shown.
- "Vendor" means the publisher sells a product in this market. Vendor numbers can be biased.

## Limits of this research

- Reddit is blocked for the search tools. No Reddit thread was read. Reddit quotes are missing.
- The web search budget ran out at 200 calls. Some pages were seen only as search snippets. The list below marks them.
- Google Trends data was pulled directly from the Trends endpoint on 2026-09-30. It is an index, not a search count.
- No India-specific data was found for: applications per fresher, time per application, response rate per application, or where freshers build resumes (phone or laptop).
- Quotes from Hacker News (HN) came through a summarizing fetch tool. Check them word for word before you publish them.

## Key findings

1. **Silence is the top pain.** Candidates send many applications and hear little.
   - India: 74% of job seekers say a company ghosted them after an interview (Harper's Bazaar India, citing People Matters). Grade C.
   - Volume: Apna counted about 9 crore applications for 14 lakh postings in 2025. That is about 64 applications per posting (derived). Grade C (vendor).
   - US: Greenhouse counted 244 applications per job in 2025, up from 116 in 2022. Grade A (vendor first-party data).
2. **Candidates say they cannot stand out.** LinkedIn India research reports 48% struggle to make an application stand out. Applications per opening doubled since early 2022. Grade B (vendor research, sample size not shown).
3. **Fresher hiring demand is mixed.** Naukri JobSpeak shows fresher hiring up 16% in FY26 and up 15% in August 2026. Grade A (vendor index). Yet Unstop reports 83% of engineering graduates and 46% of B-school graduates have no job or internship offer. Grade B (vendor report, sample not shown in the article).
4. **AI use is near universal, and trust in the output is low.**
   - 94% of Indian job seekers plan to use AI tools in the job search (LinkedIn, Jan 2026). Grade B.
   - 74% of US job seekers use AI (Greenhouse 2025, 4,136 respondents, India not included). Grade A (vendor).
   - 86% of US hiring managers say AI makes it too easy to exaggerate skills (Express-Harris Poll, Feb 2026). Grade B.
5. **Tailoring effort is a real pain. "Re-entering my background" is only weakly stated.** Sources show that tailoring each application is slow (44 minutes per application, US, vendor). No source found says "I re-paste my background into ChatGPT every time" in those words. See the section on this pain.

## How candidates use ChatGPT and AI today

Adoption:
- India: 94% plan to use AI tools in the job search (LinkedIn, Jan 2026). Grade B.
- US: 74% of job seekers use AI. 49% submit more applications than a year ago (Greenhouse 2025). Grade A (vendor).
- US students: 85% of rising seniors use AI tools (Handshake, Class of 2026, via search snippet). Grade A (vendor).
- A 2024 Neurosight survey, cited by TechStory, reports 57% of student job seekers use ChatGPT for applications. Sample size not shown. Grade C.
- A 2023 ResumeBuilder survey (1,000 US seekers) found 46% used ChatGPT for a resume or cover letter. 11% said they were rejected after they disclosed it. Grade C, old, vendor.

Frustrations reported:
- **Generic output.** Recruiters say they see the same buzzwords. Vendor sources say 49-62% of hiring managers reject generic AI resumes. The 62% figure is from a Resume Now survey (seen in a search snippet only). Grade C (vendor).
- **Invented content.** Guides warn that ChatGPT invents metrics. Grade C. The Express-Harris Poll shows employers fear exaggeration: 86% agree AI makes it too easy. 22% of job seekers admit they list skills they do not have. Grade B.
- **No memory of the candidate's background.** Prompt guides tell users to paste their history and the job description each time, because ChatGPT does not know the candidate. Grade C (vendor blogs).
- **Formatting and ATS worry.** Guides teach "ATS-safe" formats. Recruiter evidence says the ATS fear is overstated (see below). Grade C.
- **Detection.** TopResume (May 2025, 600 US hiring managers) reports 33.5% could spot an AI resume in under 20 seconds. Search snippet only. Grade C (vendor).

Evidence that AI help works when it edits real text:
- A field experiment with nearly half a million job seekers found that algorithmic writing help on resumes raised hires by 8%. It found no evidence that employers were less satisfied (Wiles, Munyikwa, Horton, NBER w30886). Grade A. The tool edited text the candidate wrote. It did not invent content. The setting was an online freelance market in 2021, not fresher hiring in India.

## Is "re-entering my background every time" a real, stated pain?

Verdict: partly supported. The pain of tailoring each application is well supported. The exact pain of re-entering background is inferred, not directly stated.

Direct or near-direct evidence:
- HN, 2026-06-18, user itstotallykyle: "I'm doing the work for EVERY application". The user describes making custom resumes and cover letters per job. Grade C.
- HN, 2026-01-23, user inesbarros1: built a tool after a friend's frustration with CV tailoring and form repetition (paraphrase). Grade C. The author sells a tool.
- HN, 2024-12-28, user submeta (paraphrase): a template workflow cut a 45-minute application to 2 minutes. Grade C. This shows the effort, and it shows a template fixes it.
- Prompt guides from Teal, Careerflow, and others tell users to paste the résumé and the job description each time (Grade C, vendor). This shows the workflow. It does not show that users complain about it.

What is missing:
- No Reddit or Quora thread was read. The blocked tools made this search impossible.
- No survey asks about repeated data entry across applications.

Action for the PRD: treat "import once, reuse for every application" as a plausible benefit, not a proven one. Test it in 8-10 interviews with freshers. Ask them to show their current ChatGPT workflow.

## Mobile versus desktop

Evidence for mobile-first behavior in India:
- 97.1% of youth aged 15-29 used a mobile phone in the last three months. 92.7% of rural youth and 95.7% of urban youth used the internet. 85.5% of households own a smartphone. Source: NSO Comprehensive Modular Survey: Telecom, Jan-Mar 2025, 34,950 households. Grade A.
- 85.1% of youth sent a message with an attachment, so most youth can share a file from a phone. Grade A.
- Only 20% of Indian internet users are multi-device (193 million of 958 million). Source: IAMAI-Kantar Internet in India 2025. Grade A/B.
- Fewer than one in ten households in Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Odisha own a computer (CMS:T 2025 as summarized in a search snippet). Grade B.
- Naukri app traffic share was 53% overall (Info Edge Q4 FY26, via search snippet). This is a market-share figure, not a device split. It is weak evidence here. Grade C.

Evidence against, or gaps:
- Semrush estimates that 80% of India traffic to Resumeworded is desktop (19.8% mobile). This is one site. Grade C.
- No source gives the share of Indian freshers who build resumes or apply on a phone. This data gap is open.
- Engineering and B-school students often own laptops. The household data may understate laptop access for this target group. This is an inference, not a finding.

Reading: phone access is near universal for Indian youth, so a phone-first app can reach them. Whether they choose a phone to write a resume is unproven.

## Contrary evidence

1. **Saturation.** Dozens of resume builders and ATS checkers already target India (for example ResumeGyani, CareersKraft, Hirend, and Scout by AgentCo in search results). Many are free. See file 02-competitors for detail.
2. **Free ChatGPT is enough for many.** Indian prompt guides say the free version is "completely sufficient" for resume and cover letter work. Grade C.
3. **Search interest for "resume builder" fell in 2026.** See the Trends table. Users may move to general AI chat. Cause not verified.
4. **Employers may reject AI-written applications.**
   - Resume Now: 62% of employers reject AI resumes without personalization (search snippet). Grade C (vendor).
   - Some survey reports say 49% of hiring managers dismiss resumes they suspect are AI-generated (search snippet, source not verified). Grade C.
   - Express-Harris Poll: 86% say AI makes exaggeration too easy. Grade B.
   - A Bengaluru founder said 65-70% of resumes he sees are AI-generated (Deccan Herald, search snippet only). Grade C.
5. **The ATS fear is mostly a myth.** In a study by Enhancv, 23 of 25 recruiters (92%) said their ATS does not auto-reject resumes. The "75% rejected by ATS" claim has no source. Volume, not automation, drives rejection. Grade C (vendor, small sample). Tools that sell "ATS score" may feed a false fear.
6. **The bottleneck may be skills, not resume text.** Unstop: 83% of engineering graduates have no offer. Vendor sources report 80% of employers struggle to find skilled freshers. Better wording does not close a skills gap. Grade B/C.
7. **Candidate distrust of employer AI is high.** Greenhouse: only 8% of job seekers call AI hiring fair. 38% quit a process because of an AI interview (search snippets). Grade A (vendor). This concerns employer AI. It may raise wariness of any AI tool.
8. **An arms race.** Recruiters say they are overwhelmed by volume. More tailored applications may worsen the volume problem. TailorMyResume must avoid "mass apply" features.
9. **Fresher hiring is recovering.** Naukri shows fresher hiring up in most months of 2026. Pain of "no jobs at all" may ease. Non-IT sectors lead the growth.
10. **Evidence is thin for India freshers.** Most numeric data is US. India numbers come from vendors (Apna, Unstop, Naukri, LinkedIn).

## Implications for the PRD

Supported by the evidence:
- **JD analysis and gap explanation (MVP 2).** It targets "hard to stand out" (48% in LinkedIn India), "no feedback" (66% impersonal), and "weak fit" (Unstop). Strong support.
- **Tailoring with only real experience (MVP 3).** It answers the hallucination fear (Express-Harris 86%) and the tailoring effort (44 minutes). The NBER result supports AI that edits real text. Strong support. Say "keyword and requirement match". Do not say "ATS score" or "beats ATS".
- **Profile import once (MVP 1).** It is plausible, but direct evidence is weak. Validate with interviews.
- **Preparation questions (MVP 5).** Moderate support (LinkedIn India: 66% say AI helps interview confidence). Voice mock interviews later have small search interest today (3.6 vs 73 for "resume builder" in India). The India value is 2 times the US value.
- **Truthfulness rule.** The evidence backs it as a real differentiator, because generic and invented output is the main complaint about ChatGPT.

Weakened or unproven:
- **Cover letter generation (MVP 4).** India search intent is weak (5.7 vs 51.8 in the US). Keep it, but make it optional for India-first users. Resume tailoring should lead.
- **Application tracker (MVP 5).** Search for "job application tracker" is near zero in India. Keep it light. Do not make it the selling point.
- **"ATS" marketing.** India search for "ats resume checker" is very high, so the term attracts users. Recruiter data says the fear is exaggerated. Use the term only in an honest form, such as "keyword match with the job description".
- **Phone-only assumption.** Phone access is near universal, but no data shows freshers write resumes on phones. Confirm before you give up a document export flow that works on a laptop.
- **Paid "application packs".** This file gives no willingness-to-pay data. See file 03.

Open questions to test in user interviews (8-10 Indian freshers):
1. How many applications did you send in the last month? How long does one take?
2. Show me your current ChatGPT workflow. What do you paste each time?
3. Have you ever received AI text that you had to fix because it was untrue?
4. Do you build your resume on a phone or a laptop?
5. Do you write cover letters at all?
